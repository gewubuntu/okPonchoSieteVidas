PONCHO: Siete Vidas · Mundo 2, el Mercado

Bestiario del Mercado

World 2's three new enemies, drawn in code at Poncho's scale (one drawing pixel is half a game pixel) in the market's colours and his outline, as World 1's four were. A pigeon of the plaza, the grasshopper of Chapultepec and a top from the toy stall: one idea each, each one a new verb for the player. Every stage plays at game speed, 60 steps a second, with the timings the game already uses. Nothing about how they play changes.

Ground:

One family across two worlds

  • Same hand as World 1. Half-game-pixel drawing, the near-black outline Poncho has, light from the upper left in three tones, big readable eyes, nobody dressed up: real animals and real toys, each with a true Mexican story.
  • Market colours only. Every pixel is one of World 2's 24 colours (the Chapulín keeps the darker leaf you chose so it reads on the pink walls), plus the outline and the telegraph red every world shares.
  • Danger reads the same everywhere. Pale points mean "do not land on me" (the Trompo's three glaze tacks, like the Zopilote's cholla spines); a 0.5 s red flash means "about to move" (the Paloma's puff, the Chapulín's wind-up).
  • Three silhouettes, three verbs. Round and puffed (wait or go under), a zig-zag of legs (read the arc), a pear on a spike (claw, then stomp). Told apart from each other, from the Alacrán and the Tlacuache and from Poncho at a glance.
  • Defeats as in World 1. A squashed or knocked-over frame for 8 steps, then the same poof.
Design for approval. In the game each frame is picked from what the enemy is doing (distance walked, phase and step), so the cycles stay in step with the ground.
